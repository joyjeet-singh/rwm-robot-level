TITLE
One Step Ahead Is Not Enough: Testing a Learned Quadruped Simulator at the Horizon It Is Used

ABSTRACT
Learned simulators let legged robots learn by running a neural network on its own predictions for tens to hundreds of steps, yet are often trained, compressed and checked one step ahead. We rebuilt the open quadruped simulator of the Robotic World Model (RWM), matched its released gradients exactly, and measured 101 trained simulators, 200 compressed copies and the released model. One-step checks reverse the training choices: teacher forcing and one-step training are more accurate one step ahead but 2.0 and 2.6 times as wrong at 2 s, consistent with RWM's ablation. Pruning 30% of weights keeps 90% of the skill one step ahead, 39% at 2 s. The released ensemble's spread is too small, increasingly with horizon, because the implemented loss is lowest at zero spread. In a registered comparison over 97 simulators, a kinematic-consistency residual ranked the 2-second error best; on a second pool it was level with an 8-step check (exploratory). The error growth rate beat one-step error but failed its registered replication on a pool of mostly compressed copies, probably because moderate pruning adds error while barely changing it. Scope: simulated data of one robot and gait, recorded commands, no policy trained. We distil a vetting checklist.

KEYWORDS
learned simulators, world models, legged robots, model-based reinforcement learning, Lyapunov exponents, model compression, uncertainty
