TITLE
One Step Ahead Is Not Enough: Testing a Learned Quadruped Simulator at the Horizon It Is Used

ABSTRACT
Learned simulators let legged robots learn by imagination, running a neural network on its own predictions for tens to hundreds of steps, yet they are usually trained and checked one step ahead. We rebuilt the open quadruped simulator of the Robotic World Model, matched its released gradients exactly, and measured 302 versions of it on a CPU. One-step checks misjudged the training objective, the training horizon, pruning and the size of the uncertainty estimate: teacher forcing is 10% more accurate one step ahead and 2.0 times as wrong over 2 s; training one step ahead yields a simulator that amplifies its own errors; pruning 30% of weights keeps 90% of the skill one step ahead but 39% at 2 s; and the ensemble's uncertainty is too small by 6.6 times one step ahead and 30.8 times at 2 s. In a pre-registered comparison over 97 simulators, the leading Lyapunov exponent, the rate at which a simulator amplifies a small error, ranked the 2-second error better than one-step error (Spearman +0.80 against +0.32) and could not be separated from an 8-step check, without using any recorded future. It did not replicate on a pool made mostly of compressed copies, because pruning adds bias rather than instability; a kinematic-consistency residual matched or beat every other check on both pools. We distil a checklist for vetting learned robot simulators before use.

KEYWORDS
learned simulators, world models, legged robots, model-based reinforcement learning, Lyapunov exponents, model compression, uncertainty
