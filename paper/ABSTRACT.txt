TITLE
One Step Ahead Is Not Enough: Testing a Learned Quadruped Simulator at the Horizon It Is Used

ABSTRACT
Learned simulators let legged robots learn in long imagined rollouts, yet are often trained and checked one step ahead. We rebuilt the open quadruped simulator of the Robotic World Model (RWM) and measured 101 trained simulators and 200 compressed copies. One-step checks picked the wrong training choices: teacher forcing and one-step training were better one step ahead but 2.0 and 2.6 times as wrong at 2 s (descriptive; partly registered earlier), also after longer training and in two of three other architectures. Pruning 30% of weights kept 90% of the skill one step ahead, 39% at 2 s (descriptive); fine-tuning restored nearly all of it (registered). The error growth rate, needing no recorded future, ranked simulators by 2-second error better than one-step error (registered, recorded commands) on 97 GRU simulators and, averaged, over three other architectures (two carry it). On the GRU pool it was not separated from 8-step error (registered) and failed a secondary replication on mostly compressed copies. A kinematic residual ranked that pool best with recorded commands (descriptive; its lead over 8-step error failed multiple-test correction), and less well under policy-chosen commands (registered). Scope: simulated data, one robot and trotting gait, no physics-simulator test.

KEYWORDS
learned simulators, world models, legged robots, model-based reinforcement learning, Lyapunov exponents, model compression, uncertainty
