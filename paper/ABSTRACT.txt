TITLE
One Step Ahead Is Not Enough: Testing a Learned Quadruped Simulator at the Horizon It Is Used

ABSTRACT
Learned simulators let legged robots learn by running a network on its own predictions for tens to hundreds of steps, yet are often trained, compressed and checked one step ahead. We rebuilt the open quadruped simulator of the Robotic World Model (RWM), matched its gradients exactly and measured 101 trained simulators, 200 compressed copies and the released model. One-step checks reverse the training choices: teacher forcing and one-step training are better one step ahead but 2.0 and 2.6 times as wrong at 2 s, consistent with RWM's ablation. Pruning 30% of weights without retraining keeps 90% of the skill one step ahead, 39% at 2 s; 500 fine-tuning iterations restored it (registered). The released ensemble's heads disagree too little, increasingly with horizon. Over 97 simulators, a kinematic-consistency residual ranked the 2-second error best, though its registered lead over an 8-step check fails correction for multiple tests; on a second pool the two were level (exploratory). The growth rate beat one-step error but failed its registered replication on mostly compressed copies, probably because moderate pruning adds error but barely changes it. Scope: simulated data of one robot and gait, recorded commands, no policy trained. We distil a vetting checklist.

KEYWORDS
learned simulators, world models, legged robots, model-based reinforcement learning, Lyapunov exponents, model compression, uncertainty
