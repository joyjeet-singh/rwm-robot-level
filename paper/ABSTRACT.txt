TITLE
One Step Ahead Is Not Enough: Testing a Learned Quadruped Simulator at the Horizon It Is Used

ABSTRACT
Learned simulators let legged robots learn in imagined rollouts, yet are trained and checked one step ahead. We rebuilt the quadruped simulator of the Robotic World Model (RWM) and measured 101 trained simulators and 200 compressed copies. First, one-step checks misjudged choices (descriptive): teacher forcing and one-step training were better one step ahead but 2.0 and 2.6 times as wrong at 2 s, the 100-step horizon RWM imagines, and pruning 30% of weights kept 90% of the skill one step ahead but 39% at 2 s. Second, fine-tuning restored the 30%-pruned copies' skill (registered). Third, checks needing no recorded future only partly replaced checks several steps ahead (registered): the error growth rate ranked GRU simulators by 2-second error better than one-step error, but was not separated from 8-step error and failed a replication (secondary); a kinematic residual ranked them better than one-step error (secondary), but less well under policy-chosen commands (against recorded-command error). We recommend checking 8 or more steps ahead on held-out episodes, with these checks as fallback. In a hand-built physics model, fine-tuning lowered one- and 8-step error, yet 13 of 18 searched simulators yielded failing policies; primary tests stayed unsettled. Scope: simulated data, one robot and gait.

KEYWORDS
learned simulators, world models, legged robots, model-based reinforcement learning, Lyapunov exponents, model compression, uncertainty
