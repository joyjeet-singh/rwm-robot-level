Nothing rescored: the 10,000-iteration models (A10k_s*, B10k_s*) already have stored scores. Three seeds per method, no intervals. One robot, one trotting gait, simulated data.

(a) Still improving (loss slope over the last 250 iterations below 0): 6/6 at 2,500 iterations, 5/6 at 10,000. Slopes are about 10× smaller at 10,000 (largest magnitude 5e-5 vs 7.6e-4). The one non-falling run is rollout-trained seed 0 (slope +7e-6). So no model has converged in a strict sense.

(b) Seed means, 2,500 → 10,000 iterations.
- Rollout-trained: E100 0.251→0.181, E368 0.335→0.201, λ1 −0.43→−0.74, r̄J 1.46→1.12.
- Teacher-forced: E1 0.074→0.076, E100 0.576→0.464, E368 2.16→0.96, λ1 0.68→1.53, r̄J 10.1→3.8.

(c) At 10,000 iterations, in every seed and on average:
- teacher forcing is still better at one step (E1) and worse at 100 steps (E100);
- λ1 is still positive for teacher-forced and negative for rollout-trained models.
All four statements hold in all three seeds at 2,500 too. Longer training shrinks teacher-forced long-horizon error and r̄J but raises its λ1; the gap in E100 narrows (0.33→0.28) but does not close.

(d) Fine-tuning control: CTRL is P trained 500 more iterations (20 X5 trajectories, a different arena from (b)). P minus CTRL mean error is 0.003 at 1 step, 0.012 at 100, 0.026 at 368. P already holds 0.975/0.978/0.956 of CTRL's skill over the floor (h1/h100/h368). So 500 extra iterations add little skill.

Caveat: "still falling" is a slope sign, not a convergence test; 10,000 is the longest length tried. Paper wording must call this descriptive.
