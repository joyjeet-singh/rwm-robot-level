Scope.One robot, one trotting gait, simulated data; the authors’ released actor; imagined return only.
Design.Study CL asked whether the two label-free checks still rank simulators by their 100-step error
when a policy, not the recording, supplies thejoint commands(the 12 joint position targets given at
every step). Its rules and analysis code were committed at 2026-11-06 12:02 UTC (commit 2cb7d24),
before any trained simulator was driven by a policy, and the job that produced every policy, noisy and
closed-loop value below started after that commit. The policy is the authors’ released actor, without action
or observation noise; at every step it reads the simulator’s own predicted state, thevelocity command(the
walking speed and turning rate it is asked to follow, held at the recorded segment’s mean) and its own
previous joint command. Each simulator was rolled out for 368 steps from the same 32 recorded history

of checks hold for simulators built on other network architectures: a multilayer perceptron (MLP, a feed-
forward network over a window of past states and joint commands), a recurrent state-space model (RSSM,
a recurrent network with a discrete latent state) and a Transformer (an attention network over the same
window). The pool has 38 runs: 26 that the earlier reproduction had trained and 12 trained for this study

A10 Training length (descriptive)
All of this section is descriptive and concerns one robot, one trotting gait and simulated data: it is not
registered, uses 3 seeds per arm and gives no intervals, and every value was computed before the studies
of appendices A8 and A9. The main models train for 2,500 iterations. Over the last 250 iterations the
training loss was still falling in 6 of 6 runs (3 seeds each of rollout and teacher-forced training) and, with
much smaller slopes, in 5 of 6 runs trained for 10,000 iterations. Longer training lowered the seed-mean
100-step error of rollout-trained models from 0.251 to 0.181 and of teacher-forced ones from 0.576 to
0.464, while their one-step errors barely moved (0.089 to 0.085; 0.074 to 0.076). In each of the 3 seeds
and at both lengths, teacher forcing was better one step ahead and worse at 100 steps, its growth rate was
positive and that of rollout training negative. In study FT (appendix A7), the parent kept 95.6% to 99.0%
of the skill of its control, which had 500 more iterations, across horizons: for 500 more iterations, on the
X5 arena, the parent lost little.
